Cheap decision models start replacing frontier LLMs for classification and rubric scoring

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, which answers up to 1,000 yes/no, multiple-choice or rubric questions about text, images, audio, video or PDFs in one pass for $0.10 per 2M input tokens, with free output. Separately, Amazon swapped Claude Sonnet for its own Strands Decider model on internal ticket labeling, matching accuracy at about 98% lower cost while running both side by side so it could roll back in 45 seconds.

why it mattersMany PMM workflow steps are really classification or rubric checks (tagging CI signals, scoring drafts against a messaging checklist), so routing those to a decision model could cut cost and add a consistent, auditable QA gate.

Managing coding agents looks a lot like managing a team, and the playbook transfers

An essay by Anthropic's Addy Osmani lays out how to work with agents: give each one a way to check its own work, state the goal and definition of done, write any correction you make twice into CLAUDE.md or a shared skill, and track quality and rework instead of the share of AI-written output.

why it mattersThis is a ready-made operating standard for the PMM skills library: a definition of done per workflow, a rule for when a fix becomes a skill update, and rework rate as the quality metric.

🛠️ try this

Assumption audit for AI-generated analysis

After Claude produces an analysis (win-rate summary, adoption numbers, competitor comparison), ask it to list every definition and assumption it used, then paste only your original request and that list into a fresh chat and ask where each term is narrower, broader or different from what you asked. Try it on one recurring report this week and see if it catches a quiet scope change.
