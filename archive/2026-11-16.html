The Neuron

One compromised MCP server got an agent to misuse its other, trusted tools 78% of the time

In security testing of an agent wired to five MCP servers, a single poisoned server hid instructions in its output that the agent then carried into calls to the other servers, succeeding 78.3% of the time. The trial count and success definition weren't published, so treat the number as directional; the fix is mapping which tools can act on which tool's output, scoping credentials narrowly and gating irreversible actions.

why it mattersAny PMM workflow that pairs a web-reading or inbox-reading connector with one that can send, publish or write to a system of record has exactly this risk, so it belongs in your guardrail checklist and in the case for orchestrated, auditable agent flows.

Nearly 90% of senior marketers use AI insights daily, but only 36% usually check them

A GWI survey of 1,883 senior marketing and insights staff found eight in ten often see wrong or misleading AI findings, more than half made a big decision on one, and 82% reported a bad outcome such as rework, complaints or lost money. Audience work was the weakest spot, and GWI sells audience data, so it has a stake in the result.

why it mattersGives you a hard number for why PMM workflows need a source-and-date rule for AI-sourced claims before they reach positioning or persona docs, and a quality metric worth tracking alongside time saved.

The Rundown AI

Two real Claude workflows: a weekly stats report and a support-inbox digest routed to each team

Rundown staff described connecting Claude to their newsletter platform for a scheduled weekly stats report with bot-click filtering, and connecting it to a Gmail support inbox, Slack and scheduled tasks so it groups the week's questions by theme and owning team and drafts one post per team for human review. A monthly pass audits ticket drivers and checks saved replies against what people actually asked.

why it mattersThe inbox-to-themes-to-team-post pattern maps directly onto PMM signal work (field questions, win/loss notes, CI tips), with the human review step already built in.

🛠️ try this

Map the trust chain of your most-connected Claude setup

List every connector one of your PMM workflows can call, with its permissions and what it can put into context, then ask Claude to flag pairings where a tool reading untrusted content sits next to a tool that can send, publish or overwrite. Put your first approval gate on the riskiest pairing and narrow that connector to read-only where you can.
