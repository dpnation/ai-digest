The Deep View

New benchmark: top agents still fail about 4 in 10 everyday office tasks

The OpenClaw Foundation's ShellBench ran 13 models across five agent harnesses on 100 messy, realistic tasks built from emails, spreadsheets, PDFs and calendar exports. The best models finished only about 6 in 10. Half the failures were omissions: the agent had found the right information and then left it out. Scores also swung widely between repeat runs.

why it mattersThis argues for a completeness checklist and repeat-run checks in every production PMM workflow, and it gives Camunda's story on deterministic orchestration around agents a fresh, citable data point.

The Neuron

Google makes Workspace MCP servers the required path for agents from Feb. 1, 2027

Google's MCP servers and command-line tool for Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Chat and Tasks are now generally available. From February 1, 2027, agents are expected to reach Workspace data through them instead of the regular APIs, which gives admins per-agent visibility, human approval for risky actions and audit logs. Google hasn't said how it will enforce the deadline.

why it mattersAny marketing workflow that reads or writes Drive, Docs or Sheets through a direct API connector should move to the MCP path before February, and the built-in approvals and audit log map neatly onto the guardrails IT will ask about.

AI agents now sign in or buy on 13% of their site visits, and fake agents grew 2,600%

Cequence Security's six-month data shows the share of agent visits that sign in, book or buy rose from 1.4% to 13% since May. Over the same period, bots posing as AI agents to hunt for credentials grew 2,600%, and nearly two in three of the fakes used a Perplexity agent's name.

Bot rules now shape who can reach your site and gated content, so marketing needs a seat in that decision alongside Security, and verified-agent policies belong in the AI guardrail doc.

TLDR Marketing

Study: 'made with AI' labels hurt ads, and explaining why makes it worse

In three experiments, 82% to 92% of people who noticed an AI label assumed the company used AI mainly to cut costs. Adding a reason, like quality or customer benefit, lowered ad ratings further. The researchers suggest keeping required labels short and keeping AI out of emotional brand messaging.

This is useful input for the disclosure section of a marketing AI policy now that states like Pennsylvania require labels on AI-generated ads.

Wikipedia explainer pages lost a median 40% of views since 2022

ShipStation's AI help desk resolved 97.5% of IT questions without a ticket

ShipStation indexed its resolved Slack help threads into a knowledge base for an AI help desk. It started by measuring how many questions came in after hours, which turned out to be about 20%.
